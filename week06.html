Ethics, Evaluation and Logistic Regression

Legal, ethical, social and professional issues run through every stage of a data science project. This week: data protection, synthetic data, choosing a decision threshold, fair and honest evaluation, and comparing models with caret.

01

A model can be accurate and still wrong

In this week's seminar, the caret k-NN model on the cancer data scored only 72% on the test set, barely better than always guessing "benign" (63%). The reason wasn't the algorithm: the id column was left in as a feature, and its large numbers swamped the real measurements. Remove it and scale the features, and the same method scores 96.5%. Small decisions about data have big consequences, and some of those consequences fall on people.

02

Data governance

UK GDPR sets seven principles for processing personal data:

1

Lawfulness, fairness and transparency: a legal basis, and people know what happens to their data.

2

Purpose limitation: collected for a stated purpose, not reused for unrelated ones.

3

Data minimisation: only the data you actually need.

4

Accuracy: kept correct and up to date.

5

Storage limitation: not kept longer than needed.

6

Integrity and confidentiality: kept secure, encrypted when shared.

7

Accountability: you can show you comply.

IdeaWhat it means in a project
AnonymisedNo way back to the person. Hard to guarantee: postcode + birth date + sex can identify most people.
PseudonymisedNames replaced by codes; the key is held separately. Still personal data under GDPR.
Informed and dynamic consentParticipant information sheets, privacy policies, and the right to change your mind later.
Data sharing agreementWho gets the data, for what, for how long, and how it is protected.
Data provenanceWhere the data came from, how it was collected and cleaned. FAIR: Findable, Accessible, Interoperable, Reusable.
Data qualityPaper vs digital forms, sensor errors, form validation, staff training.
03

Synthetic data and SMOTE

A classifier learns best when it sees a similar number of examples of each class. SMOTE (Synthetic Minority Over-sampling Technique) creates new minority-class cases: for each minority point it picks one of its K nearest minority neighbours and places a new point at a random spot on the line between them. Whole synthetic datasets (the synthpop package) can also be shared instead of real records to reduce the risk of identifying people.

Like smotefamily::sample_generator(1000, ratio = 0.7) then SMOTE(..., K = 5): in R this turns 695 / 305 into 695 / 610. Synthetic points sit inside the minority region, so they add variety rather than exact copies. Apply SMOTE to the training set only, never the test set.

04

Logistic regression, thresholds and ROC

A logistic regression (or any probabilistic classifier) outputs a score between 0 and 1. You turn it into a decision with a threshold: above it, predict "disease". Moving the threshold trades sensitivity against specificity. The ROC curve plots every possible trade-off; the area under it (AUC) summarises how well the scores separate the classes (1 = perfect, 0.5 = guessing).

model <- glm(am ~ mpg + hp + wt, data = train_data, family = binomial)
prob  <- predict(model, test_data, type = "response")   # probabilities
pred  <- ifelse(prob > 0.5, 1, 0)                       # threshold 0.5
pROC::roc(test_data$am, prob)                          # ROC curve and AUC

05

Training and comparing models with caret

library(caret)
partition    <- createDataPartition(data$diagnosis, p = 0.7, list = FALSE)  # stratified 70/30
trainingData <- data[partition, ];  testData <- data[-partition, ]
control <- trainControl(method = "repeatedcv", number = 10, savePredictions = TRUE)
model <- train(diagnosis ~ ., method = "knn", data = trainingData, trControl = control)
confusionMatrix(predict(model, testData), testData$diagnosis)
Result

Five algorithms, same data, same folds

Breast cancer data with id removed, features centred and scaled, set.seed(1), 399 training and 170 test cases. The linear SVM has the best cross-validated accuracy. Differences of 1–2 points are within the fold-to-fold spread, so several models are effectively tied.

No-information rate (NIR)

The accuracy of always predicting the majority class: 107/170 = 62.9% here. A useful model must beat it. caret reports a one-sided test "Accuracy > NIR".

Kappa

Agreement between predictions and truth beyond what chance would give. 0 = chance, 1 = perfect; above 0.8 is usually called excellent.

Leakage from an ID column. With id left in and no scaling, caret's k-NN reached 72.4% (p = 0.006 against the NIR). Without id and with scaling: 96.5%. Always check which columns your model is using.
06

Algorithmic bias

An algorithm is biased when it systematically treats groups unfairly: recruitment tools that down-rank women, risk scores in criminal sentencing, image search results for "CEO". Bias can come from the labels, from who is in the data, or from a single threshold applied to groups whose scores are distributed differently.

Explorer

One threshold, two groups

A screening model scores applicants from two groups. Group B's scores are shifted lower (for example because the training data under-represented them), although the true rate of "qualified" people is the same. Compare error rates, then try separate thresholds.

Equal thresholds give group B a much lower true positive rate: qualified people from B are rejected more often (an "equal opportunity" gap). Fairness toolkits such as IBM AI Fairness 360 measure these gaps. Note that different fairness definitions can't all be satisfied at once, so the choice is an ethical decision, not just a technical one.

FAT: Fairness (e.g. balanced groups), Accountability (a human is responsible), Transparency (the rationale can be exposed). IEEE P7003 is a standard specifically on algorithmic bias.
07

Trust, explainability and induction

Automation bias

People over-trust a computer's suggestion, even when it is wrong: in an ECG study, incorrect automated interpretations led clinicians to more wrong diagnoses. Good design calibrates trust to how reliable the system actually is.

The problem of induction

ML generalises from past examples. Russell's turkey was fed every morning, until Christmas Eve. Every swan seen was white, until black swans. Watch for concept drift: the world changes after the model is trained.

Other pitfalls

Unreliable labels, data leakage, missing data, class imbalance, overfitting, poor transfer to new hospitals or populations.

Cognitive biases

Confirmation bias, anchoring, priming, availability. They affect the analyst as well as the users.

08

Reporting, reproducibility and sustainability

Inconsistent evaluation

Different splits (60/40 … LOOCV) and different metrics (accuracy, F1, AUC, kappa) make studies impossible to compare: apples vs oranges. Follow reporting guidelines such as TRIPOD-ML, and report confidence intervals (e.g. SE of a proportion = √(p(1 − p)/n)).

Ethics in statistics

Under-powered studies, the file-drawer effect, multiple testing, p-hacking, removing "inconvenient" outliers, pseudo-replication.

Reproducibility

70% of researchers have failed to reproduce someone else's experiment. Set seeds, share code and data where allowed, record package versions, use a checklist.

Sustainability

Maintainable, reusable, commented code; interoperability; the energy cost of training large models; long-term effects on jobs and skills.

Key terms

TermMeaning
GDPRGeneral Data Protection Regulation: seven principles for personal data.
PseudonymisationReplacing identifiers with codes; reversible with a key.
SMOTECreates synthetic minority cases by interpolating between neighbours.
ThresholdThe score above which a case is predicted positive.
ROC, AUCSensitivity vs 1 − specificity for every threshold; area under that curve.
No-information rateAccuracy of always guessing the majority class.
KappaAgreement beyond chance.
Data leakageInformation the model shouldn't have (an ID, test data, the future) influencing training.
Algorithmic biasSystematic unfair treatment of groups by a model.
Automation biasOver-reliance on an automated suggestion.
Concept driftThe relationship the model learned changes over time.

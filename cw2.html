    How the group works

    Brief Groups have 3 to 5 members and are allocated by the teaching team. Meet weekly, use lab time, and keep agendas, minutes and attendance. You may use OneDrive, Teams, Git and similar tools.

    The group leader

    Brief Submits the IEEE paper for the whole group and informs the module leader and their tutor who the leader is. The brief asks for this preferably by the end of Week 4; aim for Week 2. Leading is an extra duty, not a role: the leader still owns a technical stage.

    Split the technical work Suggested

    Choose your group size (3, 4 or 5). Every member leads one coding stage, one part of the paper, and the matching part of their own video. Everyone must still understand the whole codebase.

    Choosing a dataset and methods

    Where to look

    Also useful Google Dataset Search · data.gov.uk

    Official rule: the dataset must be open, and you must not reuse data, examples or content from a previous assignment or one running at the same time.

    Is this dataset good enough?

      No minimum size is set. Choose a dataset that fits your research question, not the biggest one you can find.

      Pick your problem type

      The project must include cleaning and wrangling, statistical analysis, visualisation, feature engineering and ML modelling, in R and/or Python, following a lifecycle such as CRISP-DM.

      Ethics belongs in the method, not only the discussion

      This criterion is worth 10 of the paper's 50 marks. The top band rewards a project that was clearly guided by legal, social, ethical and professional issues, with attention to sustainability, inclusion and equality and diversity, backed by policies, legislation and standards. Show what you actually did, for example:

      Legal and data
      Licence checked; personal data and GDPR considered; sensitive columns removed or justified.
      Fairness
      Performance compared across subgroups such as gender or age band; class imbalance handled and reported.
      Trust and sustainability
      Interpretable model or feature importance; fixed seeds for reproducibility; compute kept proportionate.

      The IEEE paper

      Brief About 4 pages and 3,000 words, excluding references; content past 3,300 words may not be marked. IEEE template (Word or Overleaf), Times New Roman 10 pt, PDF, scholarly references in IEEE style. The word count goes at the end, e.g. "Word count excluding references = 2965 words".

      Required headings Brief

      1. Title
      2. Abstract
      3. Introduction
        a. Related works · b. Aim and objectives
      4. Methods
      5. Results
      6. Discussion
        a. Professional, ethical, social, sustainability and other factors · b. Limitations
      7. Conclusion
      8. References (IEEE or similar)
      9. Word count excluding references

      The Methods section should cover how you accessed the data, the techniques and packages you used, and how you evaluated and validated the models.

      Word budget Suggested

      Change the numbers to plan your sections, or enter your draft's real counts.

      3000

      Not a lab diary. Report the decisions that mattered and why, not every command you ran.
      Figures, not screenshots. Export plots with ggsave(), number and caption them, and refer to each one in the text.
      Compare, don't just report. Use a baseline, two or more models, cross-validation and benchmarks from the related work.

      Your 4-minute video

      Brief Make it on your own, without sharing or co-producing it. MP4 is preferred; don't use .wmv. Use Panopto or other screen-capture software, with slides and/or your code editor. Showing your face is optional. Content after 4:24 may not be marked.

        Show the four qualities the rubric names

        The top band rewards "innovative practice" that makes code readable, updatable, maintainable and reusable. Point to real examples in your own code:

        • Readable: meaningful names, comments that explain why, consistent style
        • Updatable: settings and file paths kept at the top of the script rather than scattered through it
        • Maintainable: one script per stage, checks such as stopifnot(), fixed seeds
        • Reusable: functions instead of copy-pasted blocks, and a README on how to run it

        Avoid these

        • Reading slides aloud, or explaining textbook definitions of algorithms
        • Showing results without walking through the code that produced them
        • Spending two minutes on the project overview (it earns 10 marks; the code earns 30)
        • Recording before the code is final, then having to record it again
        • Background noise, or uploading without checking the video plays in Blackboard
